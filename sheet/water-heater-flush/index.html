Water Heater Flush: Printable Step Guide

Sediment builds up in every water heater: minerals settle at the bottom, insulate the burner, and make the heater work harder for less hot water. Flushing it out once a year takes an hour and adds years to the tank's life. The order matters — power off first, water off second, cool down third — and the one rule that cannot be broken is never to turn the power back on before the tank is full, or the heating element burns out in seconds. The trap is the drain valve: it clogs with sediment, so have a second hose ready and be patient. This printable chart walks through twelve steps with what to do, how, and the note. 💡 Quick Tips

Never power on an empty tank: Turning the power or gas back on before the tank is full burns out the heating element in seconds. Fill the tank first, then restore power.

Flush once a year: Sediment insulates the burner and makes the heater work harder. A yearly flush takes an hour and adds years to the tank's life.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I flush my water heater?

Once a year. In hard-water areas, twice a year — the minerals build up faster. If the water runs brown or the heater rumbles, flush it now regardless of the schedule.

Why is my water heater making a rumbling noise?

Sediment at the bottom of the tank is boiling and popping as the burner heats it. Flushing the tank removes the sediment and usually stops the noise — if it does not, the tank may be failing.

Can I flush a water heater myself?

Yes — it takes an hour, a garden hose, and basic care. The critical rules are: power off first, let it cool, and never restore power before the tank is full. If the drain valve is stuck or the heater is gas, call a plumber.
