Scoring Lines Chart: Printable Cricut Scoring Guide

A scored fold is the difference between a card that opens cleanly and one that cracks along the crease. Scoring crushes a line into the paper so the fold follows it exactly, and the right tool depends on the material: a stylus for light paper, the single scoring wheel for cardstock, and the double wheel for thick or coated stock that would split under a single line. The trap is scoring too hard on delicate materials — vellum tears, acetate cracks — or too lightly on heavy ones, where the fold wanders off the line. Test on a scrap first, score on the inside of the fold, and score twice on heavy cardstock. This printable chart covers twelve materials with the right tool, the setting, and the tip that saves the project. Consult the live reference table below, or print the sheet and keep it by your Cricut.

💡 Quick Tips

Score on the inside of the fold: The scored line goes on the inside of the fold, so the paper folds toward the score. Scoring the outside leaves a ragged edge.

Test on a scrap first: Every material batch behaves differently. Score a scrap, fold it, and check for cracking before you score the real piece.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between the scoring stylus and the scoring wheel?

The stylus is a pen-like tool that presses a line with standard pressure — best for light paper and cardstock. The scoring wheel rolls deeper: wheel 01 is a single wheel for medium materials, wheel 02 is a double wheel for thick or coated materials that crack under one line.

Why does my cardstock crack when I fold it?

The score was too light for the material, or the fold went against the score. Score heavy cardstock twice with the scoring wheel, fold toward the scored line, and crease with a bone folder. Foil and glitter cardstock need the double wheel (02) to avoid cracking.

Can I score with a Cricut Explore or only a Maker?

Both can score. The Explore uses the scoring stylus in the accessory clamp; the Maker uses the stylus or the scoring wheels in the adaptive tool system. The wheels are Maker-only, but the stylus works on either machine for light materials.
