Fabric Yardage Estimator: Printable Chart

Buying fabric without a yardage estimate is how you end up short by half a yard — or with three yards you will never use. The rule is simple: measure the finished length, add the hem and the seam allowance, and check the fabric width — a 44-inch quilting cotton needs more yardage than a 60-inch apparel fabric for the same project. The trap is patterned fabric: a large repeat eats an extra half yard or more for matching, and directional prints need the same. Always buy a little extra for mistakes and shrinkage. This printable chart lists twelve projects with the finished size, fabric width, yardage needed, and the note. Consult the live reference table below, or print the sheet and keep it by the cutting table.

💡 Quick Tips

Add a half yard for patterned fabric: A large print repeat eats yardage for matching, and directional prints need the same. Buy the extra half yard.

Wider fabric means less yardage: A 60-inch apparel fabric covers more than a 44-inch quilting cotton for the same project. Always check the width before you buy.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much fabric do I need for a queen quilt?

Eight to eight and a half yards of 44-inch fabric for the top alone — the backing and binding are extra. A 90 by 100 inch quilt needs that much because of the 15-inch drop on each side.

Why did I run out of fabric halfway through?

The pattern repeat or the nap was not counted. Patterned fabric needs an extra half yard for matching, and directional prints like velvet need every piece cut the same way up.

How do I estimate yardage for a dress?

Measure the finished length from shoulder to hem, add the sleeve length and a few inches for hems — about two to three yards of 44-inch fabric for a simple adult dress, plus a half yard for a patterned print.
