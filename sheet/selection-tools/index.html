Photoshop Selection Tools: Printable Guide

A bad selection is visible from across the room: jagged edges, halos of background, hair turned to mush. A good selection is invisible. Photoshop gives you twelve tools for the job, and the skill is picking the right one for the edge in front of you — the marquee for straight lines, the lasso for rough outlines, Quick Selection for fast subjects, the pen for product cutouts, and Select and Mask for hair. The trap is using one tool for everything: the Magic Wand on a busy photo selects a mess, and the lasso on a straight edge wastes ten minutes. Zoom in, feather by one or two pixels, and refine the edge before you cut. This printable chart lists all twelve tools with shortcuts, what each does, and when to use it. 💡 Quick Tips

Feather by 1–2 pixels: A one-pixel feather softens the cut edge so the selection blends into its new background. Zero feather leaves a hard, fake-looking line.

Refine hair with Select and Mask: Hair and fur defeat every other tool. Paint over the hair in Select and Mask with the Refine Edge brush and let it separate the strands.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which selection tool should I use for people?

Start with Select Subject or Object Selection for the bulk of the person, then open Select and Mask to refine the hair. The pen tool is overkill for people but perfect for products with hard edges.

What is the difference between Quick Selection and Magic Wand?

Quick Selection paints a selection and grows it as you drag, following edges as it goes. Magic Wand selects all similar-colored pixels at once with one click — great for a solid sky, useless on a busy photo.

Why does my cutout have a white halo?

The selection included a fringe of background pixels. Fix it in Select and Mask with the Decontaminate Colors option, or shrink the selection by one pixel before cutting. A one-pixel feather also hides the edge.
