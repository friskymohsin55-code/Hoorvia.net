Oil Change Records Chart: Printable Intervals & Maintenance Log

Most cars do not need an oil change every 3,000 miles anymore, yet many drivers still follow the old rule and throw away money — or follow no rule at all and quietly wear out their engines. Modern full-synthetic oil can run 7,500 to 10,000 miles, but towing, short trips, and dusty roads cut that in half, and nobody remembers the exact date or mileage of the last change without writing it down. That is why this printable sheet pairs the right interval for every oil type and driving condition with a simple record-keeping format: date, mileage, oil grade, and what went in the crankcase. Consult the live reference table below to set your interval, then print the sheet and log every change in the glove box.

💡 Quick Tips

Follow the manual first: Your owner's manual beats any generic interval — some engines spec 10,000-mile synthetic runs, others demand 5,000 regardless.

Log the odometer, not just the date: Mileage is the only number that tells you when oil is actually spent, so record the odometer reading at every change.

Check the dipstick monthly: Catching low oil early costs nothing and prevents the kind of wear no interval debate can undo.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I really change my oil?

Follow your owner's manual: most modern cars run 7,500–10,000 miles on full synthetic, but severe duty — towing, short trips, dust — cuts that to 3,000–5,000. Your driving pattern decides, not the old 3,000-mile myth.

Can I switch from conventional to synthetic oil?

Yes, engines switch safely in either direction at any mileage, as long as the grade matches the manual. Synthetic resists breakdown longer and flows better in cold starts, which is why most new cars spec it.

What should I write down at each oil change?

Date, odometer mileage, oil grade and brand, filter part number, and who did the work. Five lines per change creates a service history that protects your warranty and boosts resale value.
