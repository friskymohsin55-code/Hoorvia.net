Relations & Filters Chart: Printable Database Guide

Rows stop being a spreadsheet the moment they point at each other. A relation links a task to its project, a rollup adds up the hours across that link, and a filter hides everything that does not matter right now. Together they turn a flat list into a system: one project page shows its open tasks, their total hours, and the ones due this week — all without copying a value by hand. The trouble is that the pieces only work in the right order. A rollup needs a relation first, an AND group narrows while an OR group widens, and a filter that looks right can hide the rows you need. Guessing means rebuilding views that show the wrong data. This printable chart lays out twelve relations and filter building blocks with what each one does, an example, and the trap. Consult the live reference table below, or print the sheet and keep it beside your desk.

💡 Quick Tips

Build the relation first: A rollup cannot exist without a relation underneath it. Link the databases first, then add the rollup that summarizes the linked rows.

AND narrows, OR widens: An AND group keeps only rows passing every condition; an OR group keeps rows passing any one. Mixing them up is how views quietly lose rows.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a relation in a database?

A relation is a link between two databases, so a row in one can point to a row in the other. Link tasks to their project, and each task knows its project while each project can show all its tasks. Most tools let the link show on both sides.

What is a rollup?

A rollup summarizes values across a relation — for example, adding up the hours of every task linked to one project. You pick the relation, the property to summarize, and the calculation: sum, average, count, earliest, or latest.

Why is my filter hiding rows I need?

Usually the filter group is wrong: an AND group requires every condition to pass, so one strict condition hides good rows. Switch the group to OR, or check for an empty-value condition — a blank date or status fails most comparisons silently.
