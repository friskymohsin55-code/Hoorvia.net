Dog Training Commands: Printable Command Guide

A trained dog is a safe dog: sit before the door opens, stay at the crossing, come when called, and leave it when something dangerous is on the ground. Twelve commands cover nearly every situation, and each one is taught the same way — lure the behavior with a treat, mark it the instant it happens, reward it, and repeat until the word alone is enough. The trap is repeating the command louder when the dog does not listen: the dog heard you the first time, it just has not learned what the word means yet. Teach in short sessions, five minutes at a time, and always end on a win. This printable chart lists twelve commands with what each means, how to teach it, and when to use it. 💡 Quick Tips

Short sessions, five minutes: Dogs learn in short bursts. Five minutes, three times a day, beats one long frustrated session — and always end on a win.

Reward the instant it happens: Mark the behavior the second it happens with a treat or a click. A reward three seconds late teaches the dog the wrong thing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to train a dog?

Basic commands take a few weeks of short daily sessions — most dogs learn sit and down in days, and reliable recall takes months. Consistency matters more than time: five minutes, three times a day, every day.

Why won't my dog come when called?

Coming to you has to beat whatever else is interesting. Reward recall with the best treats, never call your dog to punish it, and practice on a long line before going off-leash.

Should I use treats forever?

No — treats teach the behavior, then you fade them. Once a command is reliable, reward sometimes instead of every time, and replace food with praise, play, and real-life rewards like going outside.
