Ring Sizing Mandrel Guide: Printable How-to Chart

A ring mandrel is a tapered metal cone with sizes etched along its length, and it is the tool jewelers trust for one reason: it cannot be fooled by a stretched paper strip or a printer that scaled the page wrong. Slide the ring down the cone until it stops, read the mark at the ring's bottom edge, and you have the size. The trap is in the details: wide bands fit tighter and need a size up, a plastic mandrel flexes and reads large, and a worn mandrel reads small. Measure at room temperature, read the mark at the widest point of the ring, and check the mandrel against a ring you already know fits. This printable chart walks through twelve mandrel features with what each one means, how to use it, and what to watch out for. 💡 Quick Tips

Read the bottom edge: Read the mark touching the bottom of the ring — reading the top edge gives a size too large.

Check against a known ring: Slide a ring that fits well onto the mandrel. If the mark does not match, the mandrel is worn and every reading will be off.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a ring mandrel used for?

A ring mandrel sizes rings and shapes them. The tapered cone is marked with US sizes: slide a ring down until it stops snugly, then read the size at the ring's bottom edge. Jewelers also use it to round out bent rings and stretch bands slightly.

Why does my ring size change on a mandrel?

Wide bands, comfort-fit interiors, and temperature all shift the reading. Wide bands over 6 mm need a quarter to a half size up, comfort-fit rings feel looser so they size down a quarter, and warm fingers measure larger than cold ones.

Metal or plastic mandrel — which is better?

Metal is better for sizing: it is machined to exact diameters, does not flex, and lasts decades. Plastic mandrels are cheap and fine for rough checks, but they bend under pressure and can read a half size large, so never use one for a final measurement.
