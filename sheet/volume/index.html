Volume Conversion Chart: Printable Cups to ml Guide

Volume is the kitchen's measuring language, and it has two dialects: the US system of teaspoons, tablespoons, cups, pints, quarts, and gallons, and the metric system of milliliters and liters. The anchors are simple — a tablespoon is three teaspoons, a cup is sixteen tablespoons, a pint is two cups, a quart is two pints, and a gallon is four quarts. The trap is the fluid ounce: it measures volume, not weight, so eight fluid ounces of honey weighs far more than eight ounces of water. Measure liquids in a liquid cup at eye level, and dry ingredients in a dry cup, leveled off. This printable chart lists twelve units with milliliters, fluid ounces, cups, and the common use. 💡 Quick Tips

A tablespoon is three teaspoons: The most-used kitchen conversion: 1 tablespoon = 3 teaspoons, 1 cup = 16 tablespoons. Memorize those two and you can scale any recipe.

Measure liquids at eye level: A liquid cup read from above looks fuller than it is. Set the cup on the counter and read it at eye level for an accurate measure.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many milliliters are in a cup?

236.59 milliliters — usually rounded to 240 ml in recipes. A US cup is 8 fluid ounces; a metric cup is 250 ml, which is why some recipes differ by a tablespoon.

What is the difference between a fluid ounce and an ounce?

A fluid ounce measures volume; an ounce measures weight. Eight fluid ounces of water weighs about eight ounces, but eight fluid ounces of honey weighs much more — the two are only equal for water.

How many cups are in a gallon?

Sixteen cups: a gallon is 4 quarts, a quart is 2 pints, a pint is 2 cups. A gallon is 128 fluid ounces, or about 3.785 liters — the number to remember for converting recipes between US and metric.
