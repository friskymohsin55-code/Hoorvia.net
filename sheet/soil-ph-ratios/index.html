Soil pH Chart: Printable Ideal pH by Plant

Soil pH decides what your plants can eat. In acidic soil, iron and aluminum are available but calcium is locked away; in alkaline soil, the reverse — and a blueberry in alkaline soil will starve no matter how much fertilizer you give it. The fix is not guesswork: a cheap soil test tells you the number, and lime raises pH while sulfur lowers it. The trap is amending blind — adding lime to soil that is already alkaline, or sulfur to soil that is already acidic, makes the problem worse. Test first, amend second, and match the plant to the soil you actually have. This printable chart lists twelve plants with their ideal pH, the range, and the soil note for each. Consult the live reference table below, or print the sheet and keep it in the garden shed.

💡 Quick Tips

Test before you amend: A soil test costs a few dollars and tells you the exact pH. Amending blind — lime on alkaline soil, sulfur on acidic soil — makes it worse.

Lime raises pH, sulfur lowers it: Lime raises pH slowly over months; sulfur lowers it over months too. Neither works overnight, so amend in fall for spring planting.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I lower my soil pH?

Add elemental sulfur or acidic organic matter like pine needles and peat moss. Sulfur works slowly — apply it in fall for spring planting, and retest before adding more. Never add vinegar; it is a quick fix that washes away and harms soil life.

How do I raise my soil pH?

Add garden lime (calcium carbonate) — it raises pH slowly over several months. Dolomitic lime also adds magnesium. Apply in fall, water it in, and retest in spring before adding more.

Why are my hydrangeas pink instead of blue?

Blue blooms need acidic soil (pH 5.2–5.5) so the plant can absorb aluminum. In alkaline soil the same plant blooms pink. Add sulfur or aluminum sulfate to acidify the soil, and the next season's blooms will shift blue.
