Yarn Weight Chart: Printable Guide

Yarn weight is the thickness of the strand, and it decides everything: the gauge, the hook or needle size, and what the finished piece feels like. The Craft Yarn Council numbers them 0 to 7 — lace is 0, fingering is 1, DK is 3, worsted is 4, and jumbo is 7 — and a pattern written for one weight will not work in another without changing the hook and the stitch count. The trap is the name: a 'worsted' from one brand can be thicker than a 'worsted' from another, so always check the gauge on the label, not just the name. This printable chart lists twelve yarns with the weight, gauge, hook or needle, and the best use. Consult the live reference table below, or print the sheet and keep it by the yarn stash.

💡 Quick Tips

Check the gauge, not just the name: A 'worsted' from one brand can be thicker than a 'worsted' from another. Always check the gauge on the label.

Match hook to weight: A lace hook on bulky yarn makes a stiff mat; a big hook on fingering yarn makes a floppy net. Start with the hook size on the label.

Frequently Asked Questions

What yarn weight should a beginner start with?

Worsted weight, number 4 — it is the most common, the stitches are easy to see, and a 5.5 mm hook feels comfortable in the hand. Avoid lace and jumbo until your tension is steady.

Can I substitute a different yarn weight in a pattern?

Only if you match the gauge: knit a swatch with the new yarn and count the stitches per four inches. If the count differs, the finished piece will be the wrong size no matter how careful you are.

What is the difference between DK and worsted?

DK is weight 3, worsted is weight 4 — DK is thinner, with 21–24 stitches per four inches against worsted's 16–20. A DK sweater uses a smaller hook and drapes more lightly than a worsted one.
