Illustrator Pathfinder Chart: Printable Shape Operations Guide

Two overlapping circles should take ten seconds to turn into a crescent moon — but in Illustrator, beginners burn an hour erasing anchor points by hand because they never learned the Pathfinder panel. It is the one panel that turns rough overlapping shapes into clean logos, icons, and letterforms: weld shapes together, punch holes, keep only the overlap, or split art into color blocks with a single click. The trap is picking the wrong operation — Minus Front instead of Trim, or Crop when you wanted Intersect — and watching your artwork vanish or merge into the wrong color. Each of the ten operations does exactly one thing, and stacking order decides the result. This printable chart lists all ten Pathfinder operations with what each does and the job it suits best. Consult the live reference table below, or print the sheet and pin it beside your monitor.

💡 Quick Tips

Mind the stacking order: Minus Front and Minus Back read top-to-bottom — check which shape is in front first.

Duplicate before dividing: Divide and Crop are destructive — keep a copy of the original shapes on a hidden layer.

Alt+click to expand: Alt+clicking a Shape Mode expands the result in one step instead of using Object > Expand after.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Unite and Merge in Illustrator?

Unite merges all selected shapes into one no matter their color. Merge only joins shapes that share the same fill and trims the hidden parts of the rest, so mixed-color artwork stays separated by color while same-color pieces weld together.

Why did my shape disappear after using Minus Front?

Minus Front deletes the front shape and subtracts its overlap from the back shape. If the front shape fully covered the back one, nothing is left. Undo, shrink the front shape, or use Intersect instead to keep only the overlap.

How do I open the Pathfinder panel in Illustrator?

Go to Window > Pathfinder, or press Shift+Ctrl+F9 on Windows and Shift+Cmd+F9 on Mac. Select two or more overlapping shapes first — the buttons stay greyed out until something is selected.
