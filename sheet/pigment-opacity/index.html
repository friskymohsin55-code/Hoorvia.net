Pigment Opacity Chart: Printable Watercolor Transparency Guide

Layer a transparent blue over a dry yellow wash and you get a glowing green; layer an opaque cadmium over the same yellow and you get a flat, chalky patch. That is opacity at work — whether a pigment lets the paper and the layers beneath show through, or covers them completely. Transparent pigments glaze and glow, semi-transparent ones soften edges, and opaque pigments sit on the surface and hide everything below. Picking the wrong class ruins a wash: opaque paint muddies a glaze, and transparent paint cannot rescue a mistake that needs covering. Every tube's label carries a Colour Index code that identifies the exact pigment, so two brands' "cadmium yellow" are the same paint only when the code matches. This printable chart lists thirteen common watercolor pigments with their codes, opacity class, and the job each suits best. 💡 Quick Tips

Glaze transparent only: Glazing works with transparent pigments — opaque paint in a glaze turns chalky and dull.

Check the code, not the name: Two brands' "cadmium yellow" match only when the Colour Index code matches.

Opaque paints cover mistakes: Keep one opaque pigment on the palette for rescuing highlights and fixing small errors.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between transparent and opaque watercolor?

Transparent pigments let light pass through to the white paper, so colors glow and layers mix optically. Opaque pigments block the light and sit on the surface, hiding what is beneath. Semi-transparent and semi-opaque pigments fall between the two extremes.

How do I know if a pigment is transparent or opaque?

Check the tube label: most brands print the opacity class, and the Colour Index code identifies the pigment. As a rule, quinacridones and phthalos are transparent, cadmiums and titanium white are opaque, and earth pigments like ochres sit in the middle.

Can I mix transparent and opaque pigments together?

Yes, but the opaque pigment dominates the mix and dulls the glow. Mix transparent with transparent for luminous glazes, and save opaque pigments for bold passages, highlights, and corrections where covering power is the point.
