Ring Size Chart: Printable US Sizes & Paper Strip Guide

A ring that spins is annoying; a ring that will not come off is a problem. The fix is a paper strip, a pen, and one minute: wrap the strip around the finger, mark where it overlaps, measure the length, and match it to the chart. That length is the circumference, and it maps straight to a US size — no guessing, no jeweler visit, no wrong-size returns. The trap is measuring the wrong finger, or measuring cold hands in the morning when fingers run smallest. Measure at room temperature, at the end of the day, and measure twice. This printable chart lists twelve common US sizes with the diameter, circumference, and UK equivalent, plus the strip method in the tips. Scan down the US Size column to your item, then read across for Diameter (mm), Circumference (mm), UK Size.

💡 Quick Tips

Measure in the afternoon: Fingers swell by evening and shrink in the cold morning. Measure at room temperature in the afternoon.

Size for the knuckle: Wrap the strip at the finger base, then check it slides over the knuckle. If the knuckle is wider, size for it or the ring will never go on.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I measure my ring size with a paper strip?

Cut a thin strip of paper, wrap it snugly around the base of the finger, and mark where the end overlaps. Lay the strip flat, measure the length in millimeters — that is the circumference — then find the closest match in the chart to get your US size.

What if my measurement falls between two sizes?

Size up, not down. A ring that is slightly loose can be worn comfortably and resized down easily, while a ring that is too tight is uncomfortable and harder to fix. Wide bands over 6 mm should also go up a quarter to a half size.

Are US ring sizes the same everywhere?

No — the US uses numbers, the UK uses letters, Europe uses millimeters of circumference, and Japan uses its own numbers. The chart gives the UK equivalent for each US size; for Europe, use the circumference column, which matches the EU size directly.
