USDA Meat Temperatures: Printable Safe Chart

The only way to know meat is safe is a thermometer: color lies, juices lie, and time lies — only the internal temperature tells the truth. The USDA minimums are 165°F for all poultry, 160°F for ground meats, 145°F for steaks, chops, and roasts with a three-minute rest, and 145°F for fish. The trap is the rest: a steak pulled at 145°F keeps cooking as it rests, so pull it a few degrees early and let it finish on the board. Ground meat gets no rest exemption — it must hit the number all the way through. This printable chart lists twelve meats with the USDA minimum, rest time, chef target, and where to put the probe. 💡 Quick Tips

Trust the thermometer, not the color: Color lies: pink pork can be safe at 145°F and brown ground beef can be undercooked. Only the internal temperature tells the truth.

Rest steaks, not ground meat: A steak pulled at 145°F keeps cooking as it rests. Ground meat gets no rest exemption — it must hit the number all the way through.

Frequently Asked Questions

What temperature should chicken be cooked to?

165°F (73.9°C) for all poultry — whole, pieces, or ground, breast or thigh. Check the thickest part with a thermometer, avoiding the bone, which reads hotter than the meat around it.

Why does pork need only 145°F now?

The USDA lowered the pork minimum from 160°F to 145°F in 2011, with a three-minute rest. Modern pork is far safer than it used to be, and 145°F leaves a slight blush pink in the center — that is safe, not undercooked.

How do I know when fish is done?

Fish is done at 145°F, when it flakes easily with a fork and turns opaque. Salmon is often served at 125–135°F by chefs, but the USDA safe minimum is 145°F — the choice is yours, but know the number.
