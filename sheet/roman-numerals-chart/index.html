Roman Numerals Chart: Printable I to M Guide

Roman numerals run on just seven letters — I, V, X, L, C, D, M — and two rules: add values going left to right, and subtract when a smaller numeral sits before a larger one. That is the whole system, yet it trips up almost everyone: IV is four, not six; XL is forty, not sixty; and XCIX is ninety-nine, not one hundred and nine. The trick is spotting the subtraction pairs — IV, IX, XL, XC, CD, CM — and remembering they only ever appear once per number. Once those six pairs click, every Roman numeral from clock faces to Super Bowl numbers reads itself. This printable chart lists the seven symbols with their values and origins, plus the five most common subtraction pairs with worked examples. 💡 Quick Tips

Learn the six subtraction pairs: IV, IX, XL, XC, CD, and CM are the only subtraction pairs. Memorize those six and every Roman numeral becomes addition.

Never repeat a symbol more than three times: III is three, but four is never IIII — it is IV. Four identical symbols in a row means the numeral is wrong.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you read Roman numerals?

Add the values left to right, except when a smaller numeral sits before a larger one — then subtract it. XIV is 10 + (5 − 1) = 14, and XCIX is (100 − 10) + (10 − 1) = 99. Work from the largest pair down.

Why is 4 written as IV and not IIII?

Roman numerals never repeat a symbol more than three times, so four uses subtraction: I before V means 5 − 1 = 4. The same rule gives IX for 9, XL for 40, and CM for 900. Clock faces sometimes use IIII for style, but it is not standard.

What is the largest Roman numeral?

There is no fixed largest — M is 1000 and Romans stacked Ms, so MMM is 3000. The biggest commonly written number is 3999, or MMMCMXCIX, because 4000 would need a bar over IV, which is a later medieval addition.
