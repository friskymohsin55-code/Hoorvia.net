Meat Roasting Time & Temperature Chart: Printable Per-Pound Cooking Guide

Planning a Sunday roast or holiday dinner requires coordinating both total oven time and safe internal temperatures across different cuts of meat. Because dense cuts like beef brisket and pork shoulder need low, slow heat to break down collagen, while lean tenderloins and poultry breasts thrive at higher roasting temperatures, guessing minutes per pound often leads to dry, overcooked results. This printable meat roasting time and temperature chart gives you a single, reliable kitchen reference covering beef, pork, chicken, lamb, and cured ham. For every cut, you get the recommended oven temperature, estimated cooking time in minutes per pound, exact pull temperature, and mandatory resting window. Consult the live reference table below while prepping your roast, or print the high-contrast sheet below to tape inside your kitchen cabinet for quick verification whenever you preheat the oven.

💡 Quick Tips

Time Is an Estimate, Temp Is Exact: Oven thermostats vary by up to 25°F; always start checking internal temperature 20 minutes before the minimum estimated time expires.

Bone-In vs. Boneless Adjustments: Bone-in roasts shield the center slightly; add roughly 3 to 5 minutes per pound when swapping a boneless roast for a bone-in cut.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does doubling the weight of a roast double the cooking time?

Not always. Roasting time depends more on the thickness (diameter) of the cut than total weight. A long, thin 6-pound pork loin cooks much faster than a thick, round 6-pound roast, so always verify doneness with a meat thermometer.

Is pork safe to eat when it is still slightly pink inside at 145°F?

Yes. The USDA lowered the safe minimum temperature for whole-muscle pork cuts to 145°F followed by a 3-minute rest. At this temperature, pork retains moisture and a faint blush of pink while remaining completely food-safe.

Should I let a large roast sit at room temperature before putting it in the oven?

Taking a roast out of the fridge 30 to 45 minutes before cooking helps take the chill off the surface for better browning, but food safety rules require keeping raw meat out of the fridge for less than 1 hour.
