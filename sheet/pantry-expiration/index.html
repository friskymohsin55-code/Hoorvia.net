Pantry Expiration Chart: Printable Food Storage & Shelf Life Guide

Print our pantry expiration chart: 12 foods with fridge and freezer storage times, danger-zone rules, and spoilage signs — stop guessing when food is safe.

That date on the package is not a safety deadline, and guessing wrong costs either a wasted meal or a stomach bug — yet most of us judge food by sniffing it and hoping for the best. Fridge and freezer times are not guesses: raw poultry keeps one to two days chilled and up to a year frozen, while cooked leftovers last three to four days in the fridge, and the two-hour danger-zone rule decides what must be tossed no matter how fresh it looks. This printable chart covers twelve everyday foods with refrigerator and freezer storage times, the danger-zone cutoff for each, and the exact spoilage signs — smell, texture, and color — that say throw it out. Frequently Asked Questions

Is food safe after its expiration date?▼

Usually the printed date is about quality, not safety — except infant formula, which is the one federally regulated date. Use this chart's storage times and spoilage signs instead: properly stored food kept within its fridge or freezer window is safe even past the printed date.

How long can cooked leftovers stay in the fridge?▼

Three to four days at 40°F or below, then they belong in the freezer or the bin. Cool leftovers quickly in shallow containers, reheat them to 165°F all the way through, and never reheat the same portion twice.

Can I freeze milk and eggs?▼

Milk freezes fine for about a month, though it may separate when thawed — shake it well before using. Eggs must never freeze in the shell; crack them into a container first, and they keep for up to a year.
