Paper Weights Chart: Printable GSM to lb Guide

Paper weight is measured two ways and they do not match: grams per square meter (GSM) is the same worldwide, while US pounds depend on the paper type — text, cover, and index each use a different basic sheet size. That is why 80 lb cover is not the same as 80 lb text: cover stock is weighed on a larger sheet, so the same pound number means a thicker paper. The fix is to compare by GSM, which never changes, and use the pound number only within one paper type. This printable chart lists twelve papers with GSM, the US weight in pounds, the basis, and the best use. Consult the live reference table below, or print the sheet and keep it in the print shop.

💡 Quick Tips

Compare by GSM, not pounds: US pounds depend on the paper type — 80 lb cover is thicker than 80 lb text. GSM is the same worldwide, so compare papers by GSM.

Heavier is not always better: Thick cardstock jams home printers and cracks when folded. Check your printer's maximum GSM before buying heavy stock.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is GSM in paper weight?

GSM is grams per square meter — the weight of one square meter of the paper. It is the same worldwide: 75 GSM is copy paper, 300 GSM is heavy cardstock. Higher GSM means thicker, stiffer paper.

Why is 80 lb cover different from 80 lb text?

US pounds are weighed on 500 sheets of a basic size, and each paper type has a different basic size — text uses 17×22 inches, cover uses 20×26. The same pound number on a larger sheet means heavier paper, so 80 lb cover is much thicker than 80 lb text.

What GSM should I use for business cards?

Around 300 GSM (100 lb cover) is the standard — stiff enough to feel premium, thin enough to fit a wallet. Lighter than 250 GSM feels flimsy; heavier than 400 GSM is hard to cut and print at home.
