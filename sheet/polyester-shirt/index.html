Polyester Shirt Heat Press Chart: Printable Sublimation Settings

Print our polyester shirt heat press chart: sublimation temperature, time, and pressure for 100% polyester — plus pre-press, peel, and wash rules.

A sublimation print on a polyester shirt should look dyed into the fabric — because it is. The ink turns to gas under heat and bonds with the polyester fibers, so a good press survives hundreds of washes without cracking or peeling. A bad press fades in the first wash, ghosts into a double image, or scorches the shirt brown. The difference is four numbers: temperature, time, pressure, and fabric content. Cotton cannot take sublimation dye at all, and a 50/50 blend only dyes the polyester half, giving a faded vintage look. Too cool and the transfer stays pale; too hot and light shirts scorch. This printable chart lists the twelve sublimation settings and rules for polyester shirts, from press temperature to the cold wash that keeps the print vivid. Consult the live reference table below, or print the sheet and tape it to the heat press.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Can you sublimate on a cotton shirt?▼

No — sublimation dye bonds only to polyester fibers, so on cotton it washes out almost completely. For cotton, use heat-transfer vinyl or DTF transfers instead. A 65/35 poly-cotton blend works but prints lighter, with a faded vintage look.

What temperature do you press sublimation on polyester shirts?▼

385 to 400°F (196 to 204°C) for 45 to 60 seconds at medium pressure. Below 380°F the transfer comes out pale, and above 400°F light-colored shirts can scorch. Always test on a scrap piece first.

Why is my sublimation print ghosting or blurry?▼

The paper moved during pressing. Tape the print down with heat-resistant tape, pre-press the shirt flat, and lift the press straight up without sliding it. Any shift while the press is hot smears the dye into a double image.
