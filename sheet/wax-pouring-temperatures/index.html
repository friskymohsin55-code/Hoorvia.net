Wax Pouring Temperatures: Printable Candle Guide

Candle wax has two temperatures that matter: the melt temperature, where it turns liquid, and the pour temperature, where it goes into the container. Pour too hot and the candle sinks, cracks, or gets wet spots; pour too cool and it sets lumpy with air pockets. Every wax has its own range — soy pours cool around 135–150°F, paraffin pours hotter, beeswax pours at 155–165°F — and the trap is one temperature for every wax. Use a thermometer, not your finger, and write the winning temperature on the jar. This printable chart lists twelve waxes with melt temperature, pour temperature, and the best use. 💡 Quick Tips

Use a thermometer, not your finger: Pour temperature decides whether a candle sinks, cracks, or sets smooth. A thermometer costs a few dollars and removes the guesswork.

Pre-heat the container: A cold jar shocks the wax and causes wet spots. Warm the container in the oven on low for a few minutes before pouring.

Frequently Asked Questions

What temperature should I pour soy wax at?

135–150°F for container candles — cooler than paraffin, because soy sets slower. Pour too hot and the candle sinks; pour too cool and it sets lumpy. Use a thermometer and test one candle first.

Why does my candle have wet spots?

The wax pulled away from the jar as it cooled — usually because the jar was cold or the pour was too hot. Pre-heat the container and pour at the right temperature for the wax.

Can I mix different waxes?

Yes — blends like coconut-soy are common and often better than either wax alone. Melt them together, stir well, and use the pour temperature of the blend, not the individual waxes.
