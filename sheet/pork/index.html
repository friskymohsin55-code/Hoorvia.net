Pork Cooking Time & Temperature Chart: Printable Cut-by-Cut Reference Guide

Cooking pork to perfection requires knowing which cuts are lean and fast-cooking and which cuts are marbled with tough collagen that demands low, slow heat. Treating a lean pork loin chop like a pork shoulder will leave it dry and chalky, while pulling a Boston butt at 145°F makes it impossible to shred. Since the USDA updated its safe minimum temperature for whole-muscle pork to 145°F with a 3-minute rest, home cooks can enjoy tender, juicy pork with a slight blush of pink inside. This printable pork cooking time and temperature chart breaks down 12 essential cuts across oven roasting, grilling, pan-searing, and smoking. Use the live reference table below to check cooking methods, minutes per pound or thickness, and exact pull temperatures, or print the high-contrast sheet below to keep inside your pantry door for instant meal-prep guidance.

💡 Quick Tips

Two Temperature Zones: Lean cuts (chops, loin, tenderloin) are best pulled at 140°F to coast to 145°F; tough collagen cuts (shoulder, ribs) must reach 195°F–203°F to become tender.

Brine Lean Cuts: A quick 30-minute salt-and-sugar brine keeps thick boneless pork chops from drying out over high heat.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does the USDA say 145°F for pork chops but 160°F for ground pork?▼

Surface bacteria on whole-muscle cuts like chops and tenderloin are killed immediately when the exterior is seared. Grinding pork mixes surface bacteria throughout the meat, requiring a higher internal temperature of 160°F to ensure safety from edge to center.

Why did my pork loin roast turn out dry even though I used a timer?▼

Pork loin is extremely lean and loses moisture rapidly once its internal temperature climbs past 150°F. Pulling the roast out of the oven at 138°F–140°F and resting it for 10 minutes prevents carryover heat from wringing out the juices.

How can I tell when pork ribs are done if the bones make thermometer readings tricky?▼

Probe carefully between two center bones looking for 195°F–203°F, or use the "bend test": pick up the rack from one end with tongs—if the middle bends at a 45-to-90-degree angle and the surface meat cracks slightly, they are done.
