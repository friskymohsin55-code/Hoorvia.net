Rice Ratio Chart: Printable Water Ratio Guide

Rice is the most forgiving food in the kitchen until the ratio is wrong — then it is either soup or glue. The trap is that there is no single rice ratio: jasmine and basmati cook fluffy at one and a half parts water, long grain white needs two, brown rice needs more and nearly triple the time, and wild rice drinks three parts water over almost an hour. Memorizing one number for 'rice' is exactly how dinner goes wrong. What you need is the ratio per variety, plus the cook time and what each rice is best for. This printable chart lists twelve rice varieties with their water ratio, cook time, and best use, so you can pick the right rice and the right ratio in one glance. 💡 Quick Tips

Rinse before you measure: Rinse the rice first, then measure the water. Wet rice already holds water, so measuring after rinsing keeps the ratio honest.

Keep the lid on: Every peek lets steam escape and drops the temperature. Leave the lid on through the simmer and the rest, then fluff with a fork.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best water ratio for white rice?

Two parts water to one part rice is the standard for long grain white rice: fifteen to eighteen minutes of simmering, then ten minutes of rest with the lid on. Jasmine and basmati need less — one and a half parts water — because their grains are drier.

Why is my rice always sticky?

Usually the rice was not rinsed, so surface starch glued the grains together, or the lid came off during cooking and the steam escaped. Rinse until the water runs clear, keep the lid on, and rest ten minutes before fluffing with a fork.

How do I scale rice for a crowd?

Keep the ratio the same and scale both sides: two cups of rice need four cups of water at a two-to-one ratio. One cup of dry rice feeds about three people as a side, so plan a quarter cup dry per person and add one extra cup for the pot.
