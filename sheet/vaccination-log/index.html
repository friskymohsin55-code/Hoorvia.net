Child Vaccination Schedule: Printable Log

A vaccination log is the record that follows a child from the hospital to high school: which shots were given, when, and what is due next. The CDC schedule starts with hepatitis B at birth, stacks the big series at 2, 4, and 6 months, adds measles and chickenpox at 12 months, boosters before kindergarten, and the pre-teen shots at 11 to 12. The trap is the catch-up: a missed dose does not mean starting over — most series just pick up where they left off. Bring this log to every well-child visit and ask the doctor what is due. This printable chart lists twelve ages with the vaccines due, what they protect, and the note. Consult the live reference table below, or print the sheet and keep it with the health records.

💡 Quick Tips

Bring the log to every visit: The doctor checks what is due against the log. A missed dose does not mean starting over — most series pick up where they left off.

Record the lot number: Write down the vaccine lot number and the date for every shot. It matters if there is ever a recall or a reaction.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if my child misses a vaccine dose?

Do not start over — most vaccine series just pick up where they left off. Tell the doctor which dose was missed and they will schedule the next one; the earlier doses still count.

Why does my baby need so many shots at 2 months?

The 2-month visit covers the diseases babies are most vulnerable to — diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, polio, Hib, pneumococcal, and rotavirus. The protection starts early because the diseases are most dangerous in infancy.

Are vaccines safe for babies?

Yes — childhood vaccines are among the most studied medicines in the world, and the schedule is designed to protect babies when they are most vulnerable. Side effects are usually mild: a sore leg or a low fever for a day. Talk to your pediatrician about any concern.
