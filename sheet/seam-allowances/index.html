Seam Allowances Chart: Printable Sewing Guide

A seam allowance is the fabric between the stitch line and the raw edge, and getting it wrong is how garments come out a size too small or a quilt block finishes an inch short. The allowance is not decoration — it is the fitting room: a 5/8-inch garment seam leaves room to let a dress out, while a 1/4-inch quilt seam keeps patchwork blocks exact. The trap is using one allowance for everything. Quilts need a scant quarter inch, garments need five-eighths, and bags need a half inch for strength. This printable chart covers twelve project types with the right allowance, why it is that size, and the tip that makes it work. 💡 Quick Tips

Test on a scrap: Sew a test seam on a scrap and measure the finished width. A seam that drifts 1/16 inch per seam adds up fast across a quilt.

Press before you judge: A seam looks wrong before pressing. Press it open or to one side — the allowance only shows its true size after the iron.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the standard seam allowance for sewing?

Five-eighths of an inch is the garment industry standard, and a quarter inch is the quilting standard. The right allowance depends on the project: quilts need 1/4 inch for exact blocks, garments need 5/8 inch for fitting room, and bags need 1/2 inch for strength.

Why do quilt blocks use a 1/4 inch seam?

Quilt blocks are cut to exact finished sizes that assume a quarter-inch seam on every edge. A seam even a sixteenth of an inch off makes the block finish the wrong size, and the error multiplies across a whole quilt top.

Can I change the seam allowance on a pattern?

Yes, but change it everywhere or the pieces will not fit together. If you sew a 1/2-inch seam on a pattern drafted for 5/8 inch, the garment comes out a quarter inch smaller on every seam — a full size smaller on a fitted dress.
