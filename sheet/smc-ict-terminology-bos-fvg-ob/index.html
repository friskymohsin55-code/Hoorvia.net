SMC ICT Terminology: Printable BOS FVG OB Guide

Smart Money Concepts and ICT trading share one idea: price moves to where the stops are. The jargon describes that hunt — BOS for a break of structure, FVG for the gap price leaves behind, order blocks for the footprint of the big money, and liquidity for the stops waiting to be grabbed. The trap is memorizing the terms without the context: a fair value gap means nothing without the market structure around it, and an order block is only an entry zone when price actually returns to it. Learn the terms in order — structure first, then liquidity, then the entry — and the chart starts to read like a story. This printable chart lists twelve essential SMC/ICT terms with what each means and how traders use it. Consult the live reference table below, or print the sheet and keep it by your charts.

💡 Quick Tips

Structure first, entries second: A fair value gap or order block means nothing without the market structure around it. Mark the BOS and CHOCH first, then look for entries.

Premium sells, discount buys: In a dealing range, the upper half is premium — sell there. The lower half is discount — buy there. Buying in premium traps traders.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a fair value gap (FVG)?

A fair value gap is an imbalance left when price moves fast — a three-candle pattern where the middle candle's range leaves a gap between the first and third candles. Price often returns to fill the gap before continuing, so traders watch it as a future reaction zone.

What is an order block?

An order block is the last opposing candle before a strong move — the footprint where big money placed orders. When price returns to that zone, traders expect a reaction and use it as an entry. The block only matters if the move that followed it was impulsive.

What is the difference between BOS and CHOCH?

BOS is a break of structure in the direction of the trend — it confirms the trend continues. CHOCH is the first break against the trend — it warns the trend may be changing. A CHOCH is the early signal; a second break after it confirms the reversal.
