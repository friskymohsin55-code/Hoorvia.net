Steak Doneness Temperature Chart: Printable Internal Temp & Resting Guide

Getting steak house accuracy on your grill or cast-iron skillet comes down to internal temperature, not guesswork or poking the meat. Because residual heat continues cooking beef after it leaves the heat source—a process known as carryover cooking—knowing both your exact pull temperature and final target temperature is essential. This printable steak doneness temperature chart gives you a clear, kitchen-ready reference for every level from blue rare to well done, plus specialized targets for high-fat Wagyu, lean bison, and safe USDA cooking thresholds. Whether you are reverse-searing a thick ribeye, pan-basting a tenderloin filet, or smoking a tri-tip roast, keeping this beef doneness internal temperature chart by your prep station prevents overcooking expensive cuts. Consult the table below for immediate temperature readings or print the high-contrast sheet below to tape inside your pantry door.

💡 Quick Tips

Probe Placement: Always insert your instant-read digital thermometer horizontally through the side of the steak into the dead center, avoiding bone and thick fat caps.

Carryover Rule: Remove thick steaks (1.5 inches or more) from the heat 5°F below your target doneness and rest them loosely tented on a warm cutting board.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why should I pull my steak off the grill 5 degrees before the target temperature?▼

During carryover cooking, heat trapped on the seared exterior continues moving toward the cooler center while the steak rests. Pulling the meat 5°F early ensures it coasts directly to your ideal doneness without overshooting into the next temperature tier.

Why do ribeye steaks taste better closer to 137°F than standard medium-rare?▼

Ribeyes contain dense pockets of intramuscular fat and connective tissue that only soften and render fully between 135°F and 138°F. Cooking a ribeye slightly past classic rare melts that fat into buttery flavor instead of leaving it chewy.

How long should a steak rest before slicing?▼

Rest standard 1-inch steaks for 5 to 7 minutes, and thick-cut 2-inch steaks or bone-in roasts for 10 to 12 minutes. Resting allows contracted muscle fibers to relax and reabsorb juices so they stay inside the meat rather than pooling on your board.
