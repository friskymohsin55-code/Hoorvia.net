Weekly Medication Tracker Printable

A clean weekly medication tracker: morning, afternoon, evening and night boxes for 7 days, printable on Letter or A4.

Important disclaimer: This sheet is for informational and organisational purposes only. It is not medical advice, diagnosis or treatment. If you have a health concern, follow your clinician’s guidance and seek professional advice promptly. Do not change medication, treatment or monitoring based on this printable alone.

Forgetting a dose or double-dosing is easy when several medicines are taken at different times. This weekly tracker gives one row per medicine and one column per day, with morning, afternoon, evening and night boxes to tick, so you, a carer or a family member can see at a glance what was taken and what is still due.

How to Print & Use This Chart

Print a fresh sheet every Monday on US Letter or A4 — choose “Save as PDF” in the print dialog if you want a PDF copy, and set Letter or A4 to match your paper. Keep the current week on the fridge, on a bedside table, or inside a pill organiser lid, where it is seen at dose times rather than filed away. Print several weeks at once and store them in a folder, so a new week is always ready and you never have to reprint in a rush. If you laminate one copy, use a dry-erase marker for a reusable week, but keep a paper copy for appointments, because doctors often want to keep the record.

Fill in medicine names, doses and times at the start of the week, straight from the pharmacy label — do not rely on memory for a new prescription. Tick a box immediately after taking a dose, not later from memory, and write a short note if a dose is missed, late, or causes a symptom. That note is valuable at the next appointment: it shows patterns a doctor can act on, such as morning dizziness or a dose that is regularly missed on workdays. Keep this tracker as a personal record only; it does not change a prescription, and any missed dose, side effect or change in condition should be discussed with a pharmacist or doctor promptly, not left until the next routine visit. Store completed sheets privately, as they contain health information.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Should I tick before or after taking a dose?▼

After, immediately. Ticking beforehand is how double doses happen if you get distracted.

What if I miss a dose?▼

Do not double up unless your pharmacist or doctor has told you to. Note the miss on the sheet and ask for advice promptly.

Can a carer use this tracker too?▼

Yes — one shared sheet avoids two people both giving a dose. Whoever gives it ticks it, straight away.

How long should I keep old sheets?▼

Keep at least a few months, or bring recent weeks to appointments. Store them privately, as they are health records.
