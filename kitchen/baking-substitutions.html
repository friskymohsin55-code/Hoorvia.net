How to Print & Use This Chart

Print on US Letter or A4 — the print dialog’s “Save as PDF” option gives you a clean PDF in either size — and keep the sheet inside a baking cupboard or taped to the inside of a pantry door, where you will see it before you start measuring. Laminate it if your baking area gets floury, because a paper sheet near butter and eggs rarely stays clean for long. Many bakers also keep a second copy in a recipe binder, so the swaps are there when a recipe calls for buttermilk at the last minute and the shops are closed.

Use one swap at a time when you are learning. Changing butter, eggs and flour all at once makes it impossible to know what caused a dense cake or a flat cookie, whereas a single swap teaches you how that ingredient behaves. Weigh flour when you can — a packed cup can add 20% more flour and dry out a cake — and remember that liquid sweeteners like honey add moisture, so reduce other liquids slightly. For egg-free baking, flax eggs bind well but do not add lift, so they suit muffins, banana bread and brownies better than airy sponge cakes. If a recipe depends on whipped egg whites for structure, such as meringue or angel cake, do not substitute; choose a different recipe instead. Note any swap that worked on the sheet itself, and over time it becomes a record of your kitchen, not just a generic chart.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I replace butter with oil 1:1?▼

Use about ¾ cup oil for 1 cup butter, because butter contains water and milk solids. Oil cakes are moist but less buttery in flavour.

Do flax eggs work in all cakes?▼

They bind well in muffins, quick breads and brownies, but give less rise. Avoid them in sponge, chiffon and meringue cakes.

What is the easiest buttermilk swap?▼

Milk plus lemon juice or vinegar, rested 5 minutes. It curdles slightly and works in almost every recipe calling for buttermilk.

Can I swap baking soda and baking powder freely?▼

No. They are not interchangeable 1:1. Baking soda needs acid to activate; baking powder already contains it. Use the chart’s ratios only in a pinch.
