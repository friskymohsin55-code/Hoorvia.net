Sublimation Time & Temp Cheat Sheet

Sublimation time and temperature for mugs, tumblers, T-shirts, coasters, phone cases and more — Fahrenheit and Celsius.

Sublimation only works when the blank is polyester-coated (or polyester fabric) and the heat and time are right. Too cool and colours look faded; too hot or too long and they blur or scorch. This chart gives starting settings for the blanks sellers use most, so your first press is close, not a guess.

How to Print & Use This Chart

Print this sheet on US Letter or A4 and keep it at your press station, not across the room — sublimation timing is short and you will want the numbers in sight while you work. Use “Save as PDF” in the print dialog for a PDF copy, choosing Letter or A4 for your paper. Laminate it, because sublimation areas get hot and inky, and a laminated sheet can be wiped clean and will not curl from heat the way plain paper does. If you sell blanks, a second copy near your packing area helps you answer customer questions without hunting through supplier PDFs.

The details matter as much as temperature: mirror your image before printing, use sublimation paper and ink (regular inkjet ink will not transfer), tape the design tightly with heat tape so it cannot shift and ghost, and lint-roll fabric blanks first. Preheat fabric for 5 seconds to remove moisture, then press without moving the press — shifting mid-press causes a double image. After pressing, peel carefully with heat-resistant gloves; colours look dull until they cool and fully develop. If colours are faded, the usual culprits are too little time, a non-polyester blank, or a print that was not mirrored. Keep a test blank of each type and record the exact setting that worked on this sheet, because two suppliers’ “same” mugs can need different times.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Why are my sublimation colours faded?▼

Usually the blank is not polyester/coated, time was too short, or temperature was too low. Test on a known sublimation blank before blaming the printer.

Do I need to mirror the image?▼

Yes, always mirror (flip horizontally) before printing, or text will read backwards after transfer.

Can I sublimate on cotton?▼

No — sublimation ink bonds to polyester. On cotton it washes out and looks faded. Use at least 65% polyester, ideally 100% for bright results.

What causes ghosting / double images?▼

The paper shifted during or after pressing. Tape all edges firmly and do not lift the press and re-press in a different position.
